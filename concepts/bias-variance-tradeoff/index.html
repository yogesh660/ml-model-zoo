The Bias-Variance Decomposition & Generalization

The fundamental theoretical constraint governing predictive modeling: decomposing expected test error into underfitting, sensitivity, and irreducible noise.

1. Mathematical Derivation of the Decomposition

For true data generation process $y = f(x) + \epsilon$ with noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, the expected prediction error of estimator $\hat{f}(x)$ at point $x$ decomposes cleanly into three components:

$$\mathbb{E}\left[(y - \hat{f}(x))^2\right] = \underbrace{\left(f(x) - \mathbb{E}[\hat{f}(x)]\right)^2}_{\text{Bias}^2} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[(\hat{f}(x) - \mathbb{E}[\hat{f}(x)])^2\right]}_{\text{Variance}} + \underbrace{\sigma^2}_{\text{Irreducible Error}}$$
High Bias (Underfitting)

Erroneous Assumptions

The model is overly rigid and fails to capture genuine nonlinear relationships (e.g. fitting OLS to quadratic data). High training error, high test error.

High Variance (Overfitting)

Extreme Sensitivity to Noise

The model fits random fluctuations and noise in the training sample (e.g. unpruned Decision Tree). Near-zero training error, large test error.
