1. The Principle of Maximum Likelihood

Given an observed dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_1, y_1), \dots, (\mathbf{x}_n, y_n)\}$ drawn independently and identically distributed ($i.i.d.$) from a data generation process parameterized by unknown weights $\mathbf{w}$, the Likelihood function $L(\mathbf{w})$ quantifies the probability of observing this exact sample:

$L(\mathbf{w}) = P(\mathcal{D}; \mathbf{w}) = \prod_{i=1}^n P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w})$

The Maximum Likelihood principle seeks optimal weights $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{MLE}}$ that make the empirical data most plausible:

$\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_{\mathbf{w}} L(\mathbf{w})$
Why the Log-Likelihood Transformation is Mandatory

Multiplying thousands of probabilities $p_i \in (0, 1)$ precipitates severe numerical arithmetic underflow ($p_1 \times p_2 \dots < 10^{-308} \to 0$). Since the natural logarithm is strictly monotonically increasing, $\arg\max L(\mathbf{w}) \equiv \arg\max \ln L(\mathbf{w})$. Taking the logarithm converts product chains into numerically stable summations:

$\ell(\mathbf{w}) = \ln L(\mathbf{w}) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln P(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i; \mathbf{w})$

2. Derivation of Bernoulli Log-Likelihood & Binary Cross-Entropy

In binary classification with labels $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$, each observation is a Bernoulli trial with success probability $p_i = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i)$. The unified Probability Mass Function (PMF) is:

$P(Y = y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i) = p_i^{y_i} (1 - p_i)^{1 - y_i}$

Under the $i.i.d.$ assumption, the joint likelihood is:

$L(\mathbf{w}) = \prod_{i=1}^n p_i^{y_i} (1 - p_i)^{1 - y_i}$

Taking the natural logarithm yields the Bernoulli Log-Likelihood:

$\ell(\mathbf{w}) = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[ y_i \ln(p_i) + (1 - y_i) \ln(1 - p_i) \right]$
The Exact Identity with Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE)

In machine learning, algorithms minimize loss rather than maximizing likelihood. Multiplying by $-\frac{1}{n}$ defines the empirical cost function:

$J(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{n}\ell(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left[ y_i \ln(p_i) + (1 - y_i) \ln(1 - p_i) \right] = \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\mathbf{w})$

Maximizing Bernoulli log-likelihood is mathematically and computationally equivalent to minimizing Binary Cross-Entropy loss.

3. Score Function, Hessian Matrix & Strict Concavity

Applying the chain rule $\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial w_j} = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\partial \ell}{\partial p_i} \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_i} \frac{\partial z_i}{\partial w_j}$ yields the Score Vector (Gradient):

$\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} \ell(\mathbf{w}) = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - p_i) \mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{X}^T (\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{p})$

Notice the exact structural parallel to Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) normal equations $\mathbf{X}^T (\mathbf{y} - \hat{\mathbf{y}})$!

Differentiating a second time yields the Hessian Matrix:

$\mathbf{H} = \nabla_{\mathbf{w}}^2 \ell(\mathbf{w}) = -\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(1 - p_i) \mathbf{x}_i \mathbf{x}_i^T = -\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{W} \mathbf{X}$

where $\mathbf{W} = \text{diag}(p_i(1 - p_i))$ is a diagonal weighting matrix with strictly positive diagonal elements $p_i(1 - p_i) > 0$.

Guarantee of Unique Global Optimum

For any non-zero vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^p$, $\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{H} \mathbf{v} = -(\mathbf{X} \mathbf{v})^T \mathbf{W} (\mathbf{X} \mathbf{v}) \le 0$. Hence, $\mathbf{H}$ is negative semi-definite everywhere. The Bernoulli log-likelihood surface is strictly concave with zero local minima or saddle points: every stationary point $\nabla \ell = \mathbf{0}$ is the unique global maximum!
