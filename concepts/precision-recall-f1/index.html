Precision, Recall, Specificity & F1-Score

The fundamental tradeoff dynamics between positive predictive value, sensitivity, and their harmonic balance across classification models.

1. The Inherent Precision vs. Recall Tension

Every probabilistic classifier generates a continuous score $p = P(y=1|x) \in [0, 1]$. Class assignment requires choosing a decision threshold $\tau$:

$$\hat{y} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } p \ge \tau \\ 0 & \text{if } p < \tau \end{cases}$$
High Threshold (e.g. $\tau = 0.85$)

High Precision, Lower Recall

The model only predicts Positive when exceptionally confident. Reduces False Positives to near zero, but misses borderline true positive cases.

Low Threshold (e.g. $\tau = 0.15$)

High Recall, Lower Precision

The model casts a wide diagnostic net. Captures almost every true positive instance, but incurs higher false alarms (False Positives).

2. Mathematical Derivations: F1-Score & Harmonic Mean

Why do we use the harmonic mean instead of a simple arithmetic average $(\text{Precision} + \text{Recall})/2$?

The Harmonic Mean Proof

The arithmetic mean is dominated by large values. If Precision = 1.0 and Recall = 0.0, the arithmetic mean is $0.50$ (suggesting decent performance), despite the model failing completely on recall.

The harmonic mean takes the reciprocal of values, heavily penalizing extreme disparities:

$$F_1 = \frac{2}{\frac{1}{\text{Precision}} + \frac{1}{\text{Recall}}} = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}} = \frac{2 \cdot TP}{2 \cdot TP + FP + FN}$$

If either Precision or Recall drops to zero, $F_1 \to 0$.
