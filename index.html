Path 01 · Statistics and theory

Foundations

The ideas every model rests on: how models measure mistakes, how they learn, why they overfit and how to judge them honestly.

17 lessons≈ 4.5 h
Start path
ModuleData first2 lessons
  1. 01 Exploratory data analysisLook before you model: distributions, missing values, outliers, relationships and leakage, checked systematically. Beginner16 min
  2. 02 Feature selectionChoosing which inputs a model sees. Fewer, better features mean faster, more robust and more explainable models. Intermediate18 min
ModuleLearning as optimisation5 lessons
  1. 03 Cost vs loss vs errorError is a single mistake, loss scores one prediction, cost (the objective) aggregates loss over data plus any penalty. Beginner10 min
  2. 04 Loss functionsMSE, MAE, Huber, log-loss and hinge: what each one punishes, and how that choice shapes the model you get. Beginner18 min
  3. 05 Gradient descentWalk downhill on the loss surface in small steps. Batch, stochastic and mini-batch GD, momentum, RMSprop and Adam. Beginner22 min
  4. 06 Maximum likelihoodChoose the parameters under which the observed data is most probable. Where most loss functions come from. Intermediate18 min
  5. 07 Newton, BFGS & L-BFGSUse curvature, not just slope, to choose the step. Newton is fast but expensive; quasi-Newton methods approximate it cheaply. Advanced18 min
ModuleGeneralisation3 lessons
  1. 08 Bias–variance trade-offTest error = bias² + variance + noise. Too simple underfits, too flexible overfits; the art is finding the middle. Beginner18 min
  2. 09 RegularisationPenalising complexity so models generalise. L2 shrinks, L1 selects, and early stopping, dropout and pruning do the same job. Intermediate18 min
  3. 10 Cross-validationEstimate how a model will do on new data by rotating which part of the data is held out. The honest way to tune. Beginner16 min
ModuleProbability for classifiers2 lessons
  1. 11 Log-odds (logit)Probability, odds and log-odds are three views of the same number. Log-odds is the scale on which logistic regression is linear. Beginner12 min
  2. 12 Sigmoid & link functionsFunctions that map an unbounded linear score to a valid prediction: sigmoid, softmax, probit, log, and the activations of neural nets. Intermediate14 min
ModuleEvaluation5 lessons
  1. 13 Regression metricsHow to score a regression model, and what each metric hides. Pick the one that matches the cost of being wrong. Beginner14 min
  2. 14 Confusion matrixThe 2×2 table of true and false positives and negatives that every classification metric is built from. Beginner14 min
  3. 15 Precision, recall & F1Precision: how many flagged items are right. Recall: how many right items were flagged. F1 balances the two. Beginner14 min
  4. 16 ROC and AUCThe ROC curve plots true-positive rate against false-positive rate at every threshold. AUC is the chance a random positive outranks a random negative. Intermediate14 min
  5. 17 Clustering metricsWith no labels, judge clusters by how tight and how separate they are, and choose k with the elbow and silhouette. Intermediate14 min
Path 02 · Predicting numbers

Regression

From a straight line to gradient-boosted trees: seventeen ways to predict a number, and when each one is the right call.

17 lessons≈ 5 h
Start path
ModuleLinear models7 lessons
  1. 01 Linear regression (OLS)The baseline every regression model is measured against: a straight-line fit chosen to minimise squared error. Beginner18 min
  2. 02 Polynomial regressionStill linear regression, but on powers of the input, so the fitted curve can bend. The classic way to see overfitting happen. Beginner15 min
  3. 03 Ridge regressionLinear regression with a penalty on the size of the coefficients. Trades a little bias for a big drop in variance. Intermediate16 min
  4. 04 Lasso regressionA linear model that switches off unhelpful features by driving their coefficients to exactly zero. Intermediate16 min
  5. 05 Elastic netA blend of ridge and lasso: sparse like lasso, but keeps groups of correlated features together like ridge. Intermediate14 min
  6. 06 Robust regressionLinear fits that refuse to be bullied by outliers, using a gentler loss or by ignoring points that do not fit. Intermediate15 min
  7. 07 Bayesian linear regressionLinear regression that returns a distribution over lines, so every prediction comes with honest uncertainty. Advanced20 min
ModuleKernel and instance-based3 lessons
  1. 08 k-NN regressorPredict by averaging the targets of the k most similar training examples. No training step at all. Beginner12 min
  2. 09 Support vector regressionFits a tube of width ε around the data and only penalises points outside it. Kernels make the tube bend. Advanced20 min
  3. 10 Gaussian process regressionA distribution over functions. Predicts a mean curve plus calibrated uncertainty that grows away from the data. Advanced22 min
ModuleTrees and ensembles6 lessons
  1. 11 Decision tree regressorSplits the input space into boxes with yes/no questions and predicts the average target in each box. Beginner16 min
  2. 12 Random forest regressorAverages hundreds of deep, decorrelated trees. Little tuning, strong accuracy, hard to overfit by adding trees. Intermediate18 min
  3. 13 Gradient boosting regressorBuilds shallow trees one at a time, each fitting the errors the ensemble still makes. The idea behind XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost. Intermediate20 min
  4. 14 XGBoostGradient boosting with second-order gradients, explicit regularisation of the trees, and heavy engineering for speed. Advanced20 min
  5. 15 LightGBMGradient boosting built for speed: histogram binning, leaf-wise tree growth and gradient-based row sampling. Advanced18 min
  6. 16 CatBoostGradient boosting with symmetric trees and leak-free handling of categorical features. Strong defaults, little tuning. Advanced18 min
ModuleNeural networks1 lesson
  1. 17 MLP regressorA small neural network: layers of weighted sums and non-linear activations, trained by backpropagation. Intermediate20 min
Path 03 · Predicting categories

Classification

Probabilistic, margin-based and tree-based classifiers, built up from logistic regression to stacked ensembles.

12 lessons≈ 3 h
Start path
ModuleLinear and probabilistic5 lessons
  1. 01 Logistic regressionThe workhorse classifier: a linear score turned into a probability by the sigmoid, fitted by maximum likelihood. Beginner20 min
  2. 02 LDAModels each class as a Gaussian with a shared covariance. The result is a linear boundary and a handy projection. Intermediate16 min
  3. 03 QDALike LDA, but every class gets its own covariance, so boundaries can curve into ellipses and parabolas. Intermediate14 min
  4. 04 Gaussian Naive BayesAssumes features are independent within each class and Gaussian. Wrong in theory, fast and decent in practice. Beginner12 min
  5. 05 Multinomial & Bernoulli NBNaive Bayes for counts and on/off features. The classic, still-competitive baseline for text classification. Beginner14 min
ModuleMargin and instance-based2 lessons
  1. 06 Support vector classifierFinds the boundary with the widest margin between classes. Kernels let that boundary curve. Advanced22 min
  2. 07 k-NN classifierClassify a point by a vote among its k closest training examples. Beginner12 min
ModuleTrees and ensembles5 lessons
  1. 08 Decision tree classifierA flowchart of yes/no questions learned from data. Interpretable, fast, and the base of every tree ensemble. Beginner16 min
  2. 09 Random forest classifierHundreds of decorrelated trees vote. Robust, accurate and hard to break: the default tabular classifier. Intermediate16 min
  3. 10 AdaBoostThe original boosting algorithm: weak learners trained in sequence, each focusing on the examples the last ones got wrong. Intermediate16 min
  4. 11 Gradient boosted classifiersBoosted trees on the log-odds scale: the strongest general-purpose classifier for tabular data. Advanced20 min
  5. 12 Stacking & votingCombine different kinds of model. Voting averages them; stacking trains a model to learn how to combine them. Advanced16 min
Path 04 · Finding structure without labels

Clustering

Centroids, hierarchies, density and graphs: fourteen ways to group data when nobody has told you the answer.

14 lessons≈ 3.5 h
Start path
ModuleCentroid-based4 lessons
  1. 01 k-meansSplits data into k groups by alternating two steps: assign points to the nearest centre, move each centre to its points’ mean. Beginner16 min
  2. 02 k-means++A smarter way to choose starting centres: spread them out in proportion to squared distance. Better and faster k-means. Intermediate12 min
  3. 03 k-medoidsLike k-means, but every centre must be an actual data point. More robust to outliers and works with any distance. Intermediate14 min
  4. 04 Fuzzy c-meansEvery point belongs to every cluster to some degree. Useful when boundaries between groups are genuinely blurry. Intermediate14 min
ModuleHierarchical3 lessons
  1. 05 Agglomerative clusteringStarts with every point alone and repeatedly merges the two closest clusters, building a tree (dendrogram) of the whole hierarchy. Intermediate18 min
  2. 06 Divisive clusteringTop-down: start with one cluster and keep splitting the worst one in two, usually with 2-means. Intermediate12 min
  3. 07 BIRCHCompresses a huge dataset into a tree of small summaries in one pass, then clusters the summaries. Advanced14 min
ModuleDensity-based4 lessons
  1. 08 DBSCANFinds clusters as dense regions of any shape and labels sparse points as noise. No need to choose k. Intermediate18 min
  2. 09 HDBSCANRuns DBSCAN at every density level at once and keeps the clusters that persist longest. Handles varying densities. Advanced20 min
  3. 10 OPTICSOrders points so that dense regions appear as valleys in a reachability plot. Read clusters off the plot at any density. Advanced16 min
  4. 11 Mean shiftEvery point climbs uphill on the estimated density until it reaches a peak. Points sharing a peak form a cluster. Intermediate14 min
ModuleProbabilistic and graph-based3 lessons
  1. 12 Gaussian mixturesModels the data as a blend of Gaussian blobs and fits them with Expectation–Maximisation. Soft, probabilistic clusters. Advanced20 min
  2. 13 Spectral clusteringTurns the data into a similarity graph, embeds it with eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian, then runs k-means there. Advanced20 min
  3. 14 Affinity propagationPoints send messages to each other until a set of exemplars emerges. The number of clusters is chosen by the data. Advanced16 min
Path 05 · From one neuron to attention

Neural networks

From a single neuron to CNNs, RNNs, GRUs, LSTMs, tokenizers, embeddings and Transformers: the ideas behind modern deep learning, one step at a time.

9 lessons≈ 3 h
Start path
ModuleFrom neurons to networks2 lessons
  1. 01 PerceptronThe 1958 ancestor of every neural network: a single neuron that nudges its weights each time it makes a mistake. Beginner14 min
  2. 02 MLP classifierHidden layers of neurons learn their own features, so the network can draw any boundary. The core of deep learning. Intermediate22 min
ModuleSeeing: convolution1 lesson
  1. 03 CNNsNetworks that slide small learned filters across an image, so they spot the same pattern wherever it appears. Advanced24 min
ModuleSequences3 lessons
  1. 04 RNNsA network with a memory: it reads a sequence one step at a time and carries a hidden state forward. Simple, and forgetful. Intermediate18 min
  2. 05 GRUsAn RNN with two gates that decide, at every step, how much of the memory to keep and how much to overwrite. Advanced18 min
  3. 06 LSTMsAn RNN with a separate memory cell and three gates (forget, input, output) that decide what to store, keep and reveal. Advanced22 min
ModuleLanguage and Transformers3 lessons
  1. 07 Tokenization (BPE)Models read numbers, not text. A tokenizer cuts text into pieces from a fixed vocabulary and gives each piece an id. Byte-Pair Encoding learns those pieces by repeatedly merging the most frequent pair. Intermediate20 min
  2. 08 EmbeddingsAn embedding turns each token id into a learned vector, so that similar tokens end up close together. Positional encodings add the word order that attention would otherwise ignore. Intermediate22 min
  3. 09 TransformersNo recurrence at all: every token looks directly at every other token through attention, all in parallel. The architecture behind modern LLMs. Advanced24 min
04 / Inside every lesson

Six steps, every time.

The same structure in all 69 lessons, so you always know where to find the intuition, the maths and the code.

01

Intuition first

Every lesson opens with the idea in plain words and an everyday analogy, before a single symbol appears.

02

A live lab

The real algorithm, written from scratch and running in your browser. Move a slider and it retrains.

03

The maths, properly

Objectives, update rules and derivations, typeset and explained line by line.

04

The trade-offs

Hyperparameters, strengths, weaknesses and when not to use it, the part most tutorials skip.

05

Code you can run

scikit-learn (or PyTorch) snippets against the datasets in this repository, plus the pitfalls to watch for.

06

Check yourself

Short questions with explanations, and progress that is remembered in your browser.
