Path 02 · Probability, inference and experiments

Statistics

From probability rules to hypothesis tests, divergences, A/B testing and Bayesian methods: the statistics a data scientist is expected to know cold.

51 lessons≈ 15 h
Start path
ModuleProbability6 lessons
  1. 01 Probability and BayesThe calculus of uncertainty: addition, multiplication, conditional probability and Bayes' theorem. Invert probabilities to update beliefs as new evidence arrives. Beginner20 min
  2. 02 Random variablesMapping random outcomes to real numbers: discrete PMFs, continuous PDFs, CDFs, and canonical distributions from Bernoulli to Gaussian. Beginner16 min
  3. 03 Expectation and varianceThe three pillars of summary statistics: expected value as centre of mass, variance as spread, and covariance as joint linear co-movement. Beginner16 min
  4. 04 Correlation coefficientsMeasuring linear vs monotonic dependence: Pearson r for straight lines, Spearman rho for rank trends, and Kendall tau for concordant pairs. Beginner15 min
  5. 05 CLT and LLNWhy averages converge and bell curves appear everywhere: the Law of Large Numbers pins down the mean, while the Central Limit Theorem dictates the shape of sample sums. Beginner16 min
  6. 06 Joint & Conditional Dist.Multivariate probability distributions formalise the simultaneous behaviour of multiple random variables. The joint distribution P(X=x, Y=y) captures all co-occurrence probabilities. Marginalisation collapses dimensions by integrating or summing out nuisance variables, while conditioning isolates the distribution of one variable given fixed observations of another via Bayes' rule P(X | Y) = P(X, Y) / P(Y). Understanding the decomposition of joint spaces into marginals and conditionals is fundamental to probabilistic graphical models, Markov decision processes, and multivariate density estimation. Intermediate20 min
ModuleEstimation5 lessons
  1. 07 Sampling & standard errorDistinguishing population variability from estimation uncertainty: standard deviation measures individual spread, standard error measures how much sample statistics fluctuate. Beginner15 min
  2. 08 Confidence intervalsQuantifying parameter uncertainty with frequentist coverage: margin of error, critical values, Student's t distribution, and proper probabilistic interpretation. Beginner16 min
  3. 09 Bootstrap & jackknifeNon-parametric resampling for uncertainty estimation: sample with replacement to bootstrap any statistic, or leave one out with the jackknife. Intermediate17 min
  4. 10 MAP & Bayesian EstimationMaximum A Posteriori (MAP) and full Bayesian estimation extend classical point estimation by incorporating prior probability distributions over model parameters. While Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) seeks parameters that maximise data likelihood alone, MAP identifies the single mode of the posterior distribution, functioning as a Bayesian bridge to penalised regularisation (such as L2 ridge and L1 lasso). Full Bayesian estimation rejects single point estimates entirely, retaining the complete posterior probability distribution p(\theta | D) to compute posterior predictive distributions with principled parameter uncertainty. Intermediate20 min
  5. 11 Estimator PropertiesStatistical estimation evaluates how empirical sample statistics infer unknown true population parameters. The four fundamental criteria of estimator quality are Bias (systematic deviation from the true parameter), Consistency (convergence in probability to the truth as sample size approaches infinity), Efficiency (achieving the lowest possible variance among unbiased estimators), and Sufficiency. The Cramér–Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) establishes the theoretical lower limit on estimator variance, governed by the inverse of the Fisher Information matrix I(\theta)^{-1}. Intermediate20 min
ModuleHypothesis testing11 lessons
  1. 12 Hypothesis Tests & p-ValuesEstablish null and alternative hypotheses, choose test statistics, and compute p-values to make principled scientific decisions under uncertainty. Beginner16 min
  2. 13 Errors, Power and Sample SizeMaster the trade-off between false alarms (Type I error) and missed detections (Type II error). Size experiments to guarantee adequate statistical power. Intermediate18 min
  3. 14 z-Test and Student's t-TestsCompare population means against benchmarks or between independent and paired groups using normal z-tests and Student's and Welch's t-tests. Beginner16 min
  4. 15 Chi-Square TestsTest categorical distributions using goodness-of-fit and contingency table tests of independence, comparing observed counts against expected frequencies. Intermediate16 min
  5. 16 ANOVA and Post-Hoc TestsCompare means across three or more groups simultaneously with the F-test, and pinpoint specific differences using Tukey's HSD post-hoc procedure. Intermediate16 min
  6. 17 Non-Parametric Rank TestsReplace fragile parametric assumptions with robust rank-based inference for skewed, heavy-tailed or ordinal data. Intermediate18 min
  7. 18 Normality TestsVerify whether data satisfies Gaussian assumptions using the correlation-based Shapiro–Wilk test, empirical CDF Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, and tail-sensitive Anderson–Darling test. Intermediate17 min
  8. 19 Multiple Testing CorrectionsControl family-wise error rates and false discovery rates across multiple hypothesis tests using Bonferroni, Holm step-down, and Benjamini–Hochberg procedures. Intermediate18 min
  9. 20 Effect Sizes: Cohen's d & VQuantify the practical magnitude of experimental findings beyond mere p-values using standardised metrics for continuous and categorical differences. Beginner15 min
  10. 21 Permutation TestsPermutation tests (also called randomisation or exact re-randomisation tests) are non-parametric hypothesis tests that calculate exact, distribution-free p-values by exhaustively or randomly shuffling group labels under the null hypothesis. Unlike classical t-tests and ANOVA that rely on asymptotic central limit theorems and strict normality assumptions, permutation tests require only that observations are exchangeable under the null hypothesis H_0. They can evaluate arbitrary test statistics—including medians, trimmed means, ratios, and custom business metrics—with exact finite-sample Type I error guarantees. Intermediate18 min
  11. 22 LR, Wald & Score TestsThe "Holy Trinity" of classical parametric hypothesis testing consists of the Likelihood-Ratio (LR) test, the Wald test, and the Score (Lagrange Multiplier / Rao) test. Derived from the geometry of the log-likelihood function \ell(\theta), all three tests evaluate null hypotheses of the form H_0: \theta = \theta_0 (or nested model restrictions) and share identical asymptotic chi-squared (\chi^2) distributions under the null. However, they differ radically in computational cost: the Wald test requires fitting only the unconstrained model; the Score test requires fitting only the constrained model under H_0; and the Likelihood-Ratio test requires fitting both models, achieving optimal finite-sample invariance. Intermediate20 min
ModuleInformation theory6 lessons
  1. 23 Entropy and Cross-EntropyQuantify information content, uncertainty and coding cost with Shannon entropy, and optimise probabilistic classifiers using cross-entropy loss. Beginner16 min
  2. 24 Kullback–Leibler DivergenceMeasure the informational divergence and excess code length between probability distributions with relative entropy. Intermediate17 min
  3. 25 Jensen–Shannon DivergenceEstablish symmetric, bounded statistical distances between probability distributions using mixture averages and the square-root metric property. Intermediate16 min
  4. 26 Mutual InformationMeasure linear and non-linear dependence between random variables using information gain and entropy reduction. Intermediate16 min
  5. 27 Wasserstein DistanceThe Wasserstein distance (also known as the Earth Mover's Distance) measures the minimum work required to transform one probability distribution into another, defined as mass multiplied by physical ground distance. Unlike Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence and Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence, which collapse to infinite or constant values when probability distributions have non-overlapping supports, the Wasserstein distance provides a continuous, meaningful, and differentiable metric that reflects geometry in the underlying metric space. This geometric property makes the Wasserstein-1 metric fundamental to Optimal Transport, generative adversarial networks (WGAN), and domain adaptation. Intermediate20 min
  6. 28 TV, Hellinger & BhattacharyyaTotal Variation distance, Hellinger distance, and Bhattacharyya distance form the foundational geometric family of probability metrics grounded in statistical distinguishability and f-divergences. While Total Variation quantifies the maximum possible difference in probability assigned to any measurable event, Hellinger distance provides a true metric on the unit Hilbert sphere via square-root density mappings, obeying the triangle inequality and establishing tight bounds on minimax estimation error via Le Cam's inequalities. The Bhattacharyya distance connects these metrics to optimal Bayes classification error bounds. Intermediate20 min
ModuleRegression inference6 lessons
  1. 29 OLS Inference: t, F & R-SquaredConduct rigorous hypothesis tests on regression coefficients with t-tests, evaluate overall model significance with the F-test, and penalise overfitting with adjusted R². Beginner18 min
  2. 30 Residuals & HeteroscedasticityDiagnose regression failure modes with residual plots, detect non-constant variance using the Breusch–Pagan test, and correct inference with robust standard errors. Intermediate18 min
  3. 31 Multicollinearity & VIFDiagnose redundant predictor correlations in regression models using the Variance Inflation Factor and condition indices. Intermediate17 min
  4. 32 Generalised Linear ModelsExtend linear regression beyond Gaussian errors to binary, count and skewed outcomes using the exponential dispersion family and link functions. Intermediate18 min
  5. 33 Logistic Regression InferenceStatistical inference in logistic regression moves beyond binary classification accuracy to evaluate parameter uncertainty, hypothesis testing, and quantitative risk interpretation. Because logistic regression models the log-odds (logit) of an outcome as a linear combination of predictors \ln(\frac{p}{1-p}) = \mathbf{x}^\top \boldsymbol{\beta}, exponentiating coefficients yields Odds Ratios (\text{OR} = \exp(\beta_j)). Hypothesis tests rely on the asymptotic normality of maximum likelihood estimators via Wald z-tests, profile likelihood confidence intervals, and likelihood-ratio deviance tests. Intermediate20 min
  6. 34 Mixed-Effects ModelsLinear Mixed-Effects Models (LMMs / hierarchical linear models) extend classical regression to handle clustered, grouped, longitudinal, and repeated-measures data by simultaneously estimating fixed effects (population-level averages) and random effects (cluster-specific deviations drawn from a probability distribution). By pooling information across groups through empirical Bayes shrinkage ("partial pooling"), mixed models avoid the dual pitfalls of complete pooling (ignoring cluster structure) and no pooling (overfitting isolated groups), providing unbiased variance components and robust inference under non-independent errors. Intermediate20 min
ModuleExperiments and causality7 lessons
  1. 35 A/B Testing End-to-EndDesign, run and analyse randomised controlled online experiments from sample sizing to hypothesis testing and guardrail metrics. Beginner18 min
  2. 36 Simpson's Paradox & ConfoundingDiscover how hidden confounding variables can reverse statistical conclusions between aggregated data and subgroup strata. Intermediate17 min
  3. 37 Sequential Testing & PeekingContinuous monitoring ("peeking") at incoming experimental data in classical fixed-horizon A/B testing inflates False Positive Rates (Type I error) from a nominal 5% to over 30–40%. Because the test statistic performs a random walk across time, repeatedly evaluating whether p < 0.05 creates multiple testing across time, virtually guaranteeing that chance fluctuations will trigger a false rejection. Sequential testing frameworks—including Group Sequential Designs (O'Brien-Fleming alpha spending), Wald's Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT), and Always-Valid p-values via martingales (mSPRT)—allow continuous experiment evaluation and early stopping while strictly controlling overall Type I error. Intermediate20 min
  4. 38 Bayesian A/B TestingBayesian A/B testing reformulates online experimentation from binary null-hypothesis significance testing (p-values) into direct posterior probability assessment. Instead of asking "Is there a statistically significant difference under the assumption that the variants are identical?", Bayesian testing answers the intuitive questions business stakeholders actually care about: "What is the probability that Variant B is better than Variant A?" (P(B > A)) and "What is the expected financial loss if I choose Variant B and I am wrong?" (Expected Loss / Bayes Risk). This framework naturally integrates historical priors and supports continuous decision-making without multiple testing penalties. Intermediate20 min
  5. 39 Causal Graphs & AdjustmentCausal Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) and Pearl's structural causal framework provide the mathematical language to differentiate mere statistical correlation from genuine causal effect. By formalising causal assumptions as directional graphical models (X -> Y), causal DAGs systematically identify confounding, mediation, and collider structures. The Backdoor Criterion and d-separation establish exactly which set of covariates Z must be controlled for—and crucially, which variables must NOT be conditioned on (to avoid collider stratification bias)—to eliminate spurious confounding and identify causal effects from observational data. Intermediate20 min
  6. 40 Propensity Scores & MatchingPropensity score methods estimate causal treatment effects from observational data by addressing non-random treatment assignment and covariate imbalance. The propensity score e(\mathbf{x}) = P(D = 1 | \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{x}) acts as a balancing score: conditional on e(\mathbf{x}), the distribution of measured baseline covariates \mathbf{X} is statistically independent of treatment assignment D. By pairing, stratifying, or weighting units via Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW), propensity methods emulate randomised controlled trials, isolating the Average Treatment Effect (ATE) and Average Treatment Effect on the Treated (ATT) under the assumption of unconfoundedness. Intermediate20 min
  7. 41 Difference-in-DifferencesDifference-in-Differences (DiD) is a quasi-experimental econometric methodology that estimates causal effects by comparing the pre- and post-treatment changes in outcomes between a treatment group and an untreated control group. By subtracting the control group's temporal trend from the treatment group's observed change, DiD eliminates both time-invariant unobserved confounders (e.g. fixed regional culture or baseline ability) and shared macro-temporal shocks (e.g. economic recessions or nationwide seasonal trends). The foundational cornerstone of DiD validity is the Parallel Trends Assumption. Intermediate20 min
ModuleBayesian and stochastic5 lessons
  1. 42 Beta–Binomial ConjugacyUpdate probability beliefs in closed form by combining Beta prior distributions with Binomial observations. Intermediate17 min
  2. 43 MCMC: Metropolis & GibbsMarkov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms enable numerical sampling from complex, high-dimensional posterior probability distributions whose normalising constants (evidence integrals) are analytically intractable. The Metropolis-Hastings (MH) algorithm constructs an ergodic Markov chain whose stationary distribution equals the target posterior by proposing candidate states from an arbitrary proposal distribution and accepting them with a calculated acceptance probability that satisfies detailed balance. Gibbs sampling specialises Metropolis-Hastings by iteratively sampling each variable conditioned on all remaining parameters, achieving a guaranteed 100% acceptance rate. Intermediate20 min
  3. 44 Hamiltonian Monte CarloHamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) and the No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS) overcome the random-walk diffusion limits of classical Metropolis-Hastings by transforming Bayesian parameter sampling into physical Hamiltonian dynamics. By augmenting target parameters (position) with auxiliary momentum vectors, HMC simulates frictionless motion across the negative log-posterior potential energy surface using symplectic numerical integrators (leapfrog algorithm). This physical trajectory simulation enables large, distant state transitions with acceptance rates approaching 100%, scaling Bayesian inference to models with thousands of correlated parameters. Intermediate20 min
  4. 45 Variational inferenceTurn intractable posterior integration into continuous optimisation: fit a simpler tractable distribution q(z) to the true posterior p(z|x) by maximising the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). Advanced18 min
  5. 46 Markov chainsModel stochastic state transitions where the future depends only on the present: understand transition matrices, Chapman–Kolmogorov equations, ergodicity, and the stationary distribution \pi = \pi P. Intermediate16 min
ModuleTime series and survival5 lessons
  1. 47 Stationarity, ACF and PACFWeak stationarity ensures that mean, variance, and autocovariance remain invariant over time, while the ACF and PACF reveal the memory structure of temporal data. Intermediate16 min
  2. 48 ARIMA and SARIMAARIMA combines autoregression, differencing, and moving-average error shocks into a flexible framework for non-seasonal and seasonal time-series forecasting. Intermediate18 min
  3. 49 Exponential smoothing (ETS)Exponential smoothing generates forecasts using geometrically decaying weights on past data, generalised by the ETS state-space framework into formal statistical likelihood models. Intermediate16 min
  4. 50 Kalman filterTrack hidden dynamical states from noisy sequential observations: understand linear Gaussian state-space systems, the two-phase Predict–Update recursion, and optimal Kalman gain. Advanced18 min
  5. 51 Survival analysisAnalyse time-to-event outcomes subject to right-censoring: master survival functions, the Kaplan–Meier non-parametric estimator, the log-rank test, and semi-parametric Cox proportional hazards modelling. Intermediate18 min

ModuleTrees and ensembles5 lessons
  1. 08 Decision tree classifierA flowchart of yes/no questions learned from data. Interpretable, fast, and the base of every tree ensemble. Beginner16 min
  2. 09 Random forest classifierHundreds of decorrelated trees vote. Robust, accurate and hard to break: the default tabular classifier. Intermediate16 min

Clustering

ModuleCentroid-based4 lessons
  1. 01 k-meansSplits data into k groups by alternating two steps: assign points to the nearest centre, move each centre to its points’ mean. Beginner16 min
  2. 02 k-means++A smarter way to choose starting centres: spread them out in proportion to squared distance. Better and faster k-means. Intermediate12 min
  3. 03 k-medoidsLike k-means, but every centre must be an actual data point. More robust to outliers and works with any distance. Intermediate14 min
  4. 04 Fuzzy c-meansEvery point belongs to every cluster to some degree. Useful when boundaries between groups are genuinely blurry. Intermediate14 min
ModuleHierarchical3 lessons
  1. 05 Agglomerative clusteringStarts with every point alone and repeatedly merges the two closest clusters, building a tree (dendrogram) of the whole hierarchy. Intermediate18 min
  2. 06 Divisive clusteringTop-down: start with one cluster and keep splitting the worst one in two, usually with 2-means. Intermediate12 min
  3. 07 BIRCHCompresses a huge dataset into a tree of small summaries in one pass, then clusters the summaries. Advanced14 min
ModuleDensity-based4 lessons
  1. 08 DBSCANFinds clusters as dense regions of any shape and labels sparse points as noise. No need to choose k. Intermediate18 min
  2. 09 HDBSCANRuns DBSCAN at every density level at once and keeps the clusters that persist longest. Handles varying densities. Advanced20 min
  3. 10 OPTICSOrders points so that dense regions appear as valleys in a reachability plot. Read clusters off the plot at any density. Advanced16 min
  4. 11 Mean shiftEvery point climbs uphill on the estimated density until it reaches a peak. Points sharing a peak form a cluster. Intermediate14 min
ModuleProbabilistic and graph-based3 lessons
  1. 12 Gaussian mixturesModels the data as a blend of Gaussian blobs and fits them with Expectation–Maximisation. Soft, probabilistic clusters. Advanced20 min
  2. 13 Spectral clusteringTurns the data into a similarity graph, embeds it with eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian, then runs k-means there. Advanced20 min
  3. 14 Affinity propagationPoints send messages to each other until a set of exemplars emerges. The number of clusters is chosen by the data. Advanced16 min

04 / Inside every lesson

Six steps, every time.

The same structure in all 121 lessons, so you always know where to find the intuition, the maths and the code.

01

Intuition first

Every lesson opens with the idea in plain words and an everyday analogy, before a single symbol appears.

02

A live lab

The real algorithm, written from scratch and running in your browser. Move a slider and it retrains.

03

The maths, properly

Objectives, update rules and derivations, typeset and explained line by line.

04

The trade-offs

Hyperparameters, strengths, weaknesses and when not to use it, the part most tutorials skip.

05

Code you can run

scikit-learn (or PyTorch) snippets against the datasets in this repository, plus the pitfalls to watch for.

06

Check yourself

Short questions with explanations, and progress that is remembered in your browser.
